Our approach

Connect the project.
Explain the signal.

Our approach is built around an execution-risk ontology: a shared model of a project’s milestones, actors, dependencies and changes. We are developing this model to connect information across reports, procurement records and funding activity, while keeping the source of each finding visible.

01 / STRUCTURE

Give evidence a shared context.

Organise project information around the programme, project, delivery organisation, procurement event and milestone it describes. Keep dates, changes and source references attached.

02 / CONNECT

Follow the dependencies.

Link actors to their responsibilities and events to the milestones they affect. Examine repeated slippage, procurement blockages, coordination bottlenecks and missing progress evidence together.

03 / EXPLAIN

Make each flag reviewable.

Our design calls for every flag to show its trigger, input values, source and snapshot date, affected milestone or actor, and a plain-language explanation. Data gaps and confidence indications belong alongside the finding.

04 / REVIEW

Keep judgement with the team.

Help officers investigate the evidence, decide what needs attention and prepare a structured risk summary. The team decides which intervention to take.

What do we mean by “ontology”?

A consistent vocabulary for the things in a project, their attributes and the relationships between them. This structure is intended to make evidence comparable and help explain why a signal matters in its project context.

This describes the approach guiding Izere’s development. The public demo uses illustrative rules; the connected model and its usefulness will be tested through pilots.
