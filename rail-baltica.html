Published findings

Cost estimates changed.
Design uncertainty remained.

Rail Baltica is intended to connect the Baltic states to the wider European rail network. The audit documents substantial revisions in the cost and delivery picture.

Original estimate · €4.648bn
Revised estimate · €18.189bn

The audit attributes around half of the reported cost increase in the promoter’s analysis to insufficient maturity and detail in earlier estimates. Scope and design changes also contributed. It notes mature design studies covered only one third of the route, leaving uncertainty around the revised estimate.

The report also describes phased delivery: an initial single-track railway targeted for 2030 and a fuller second phase without a precise timetable.

Izere’s interpretation

Three questions for
an earlier project review.

These are examples of how we would structure a review using our approach. They are not historical alerts generated by Izere.

01 / Estimate maturity

What supports the budget?

Review how much of the estimate rests on mature design and where assumptions remain provisional.

Connected evidence

Link each estimate version to its design package, assumptions and approval date. Highlight what still needs validation before a budget commitment.

02 / Scope changes

What changed—and what depends on it?

Review design and scope changes together with their potential cost and schedule implications.

Connected evidence

Connect change records to affected contracts, estimates and milestones. Ask the responsible team to confirm whether downstream plans have been updated.

03 / Delivery phases

What does each phase actually deliver?

Make the intended output of each phase explicit, together with the dependencies needed to make it useful.

Connected evidence

Link phase objectives to milestones and interfaces. Review unresolved dependencies and document who will confirm the next decision.

The lesson for Izere: connect evidence about maturity, changes and dependencies so people can investigate it together. This case does not establish that Izere would have predicted or prevented the cost increase. That requires prospective pilot validation.
